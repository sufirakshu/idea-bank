## Origin

This idea emerged from the broader Wild Womaniya approach to preserving ideas and allowing them to continue developing through interaction with readers.

## The Problem

Ideas can change after they are published.

Readers may respond to an idea, interpret it differently, add examples, challenge assumptions, or develop related ideas.

A static publication does not naturally preserve that ongoing evolution.

## The Idea

Create a living library in which ideas remain documented while reader responses, interpretations, additions, and related developments can become part of the continuing record.

The library would therefore function as more than a static collection of finished publications.

## Why This Idea Exists

The concept explores how published ideas can remain open to further development rather than being treated as permanently finished once released.

## Who Is It For?

### Primary

Readers and people interacting with published ideas.

### Secondary

Creators and contributors who want to document responses or developments related to existing ideas.

The library is intended to preserve not only the original idea but also its continuing relationship with readers and subsequent developments.

## Minimum Viable Version

A minimum version could consist of:

1. A published idea.
2. A place to record reader responses.
3. A way to associate each response with the relevant idea.
4. A record of subsequent developments.

The exact implementation was not defined.

## Open Questions

- How should reader responses be submitted?
- Who decides which responses become part of the permanent record?
- Should contributors receive attribution?
- Should responses be displayed alongside the original idea?
- How should disagreements or contradictory interpretations be documented?
- Should the library be chronological, topic-based, or both?
- What platform should host it?

## Risks / Limitations

A living library could become difficult to organize as the number of ideas and responses grows.

The distinction between the original idea and later contributions would need to remain clear.
