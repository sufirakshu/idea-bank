## Origin

This idea developed from discussions about consistency, unfinished work, and returning to a project after stepping away from it.

## The Problem

A person may stop working on something because they missed a day, lost momentum, became overwhelmed, or could not maintain a previously intended routine.

A perfection-based approach can make restarting feel like failure.

## The Idea

Prioritize **re-entry over perfection**.

Instead of treating a break or interruption as the end of a project or practice, make returning to it a normal part of the process.

The important action is to re-enter rather than to restore a perfect streak.

## Why This Idea Exists

The concept emerged from the recognition that continuity does not always mean uninterrupted activity.

A system can remain usable even when the person leaves it and returns later.

## Who Is It For?

It treats returning after an interruption as a valid part of the process rather than requiring uninterrupted consistency.

## Minimum Viable Version

A minimum version is the principle itself:

**Re-entry over perfection.**

A person who has stopped can simply return to the work without needing to recreate the conditions of the previous streak.

The principle does not eliminate the practical consequences of interruptions.

It is a conceptual approach to returning to work rather than a guarantee of continued progress.
