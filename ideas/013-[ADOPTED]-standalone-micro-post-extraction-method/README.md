## Origin

This idea developed from the process of turning longer conversations, ideas, and written material into individual short-form pieces.

## The Problem

Long-form material can contain many smaller ideas that can stand independently.

Without a deliberate extraction process, those smaller ideas can remain buried inside the original material.

## The Idea

Extract individual, genuinely distinct ideas from longer material and turn them into standalone micro-posts.

Each extracted post should be able to make sense independently rather than functioning only as a fragment of the original source.

## Why This Idea Exists

The method provides a way to reuse existing thinking and writing without simply repeating the original long-form material.

## Who Is It For?

### Primary

Creators who produce long-form writing, conversations, notes, or other substantial material and want to identify standalone short-form ideas within it.

### Secondary

Not defined.

## Context

The method developed within Wild Womaniya's content and publishing ecosystem.

It is related to the broader idea of documenting and preserving individual ideas.

## What Already Exists

The extraction method was used as a content-development approach.

## What Makes This Idea Distinct

The focus is on identifying genuinely independent ideas rather than simply cutting a long text into arbitrary shorter sections.

## What Has Already Been Done

- The method was developed.
- Longer material was treated as a source from which standalone ideas could be extracted.
- The extracted ideas could then become independent micro-posts.

## What Has NOT Been Done

No formal automated software implementation of the method has been documented.

## Minimum Viable Version

A minimum version consists of:

1. A source conversation or long-form text.
2. Identification of distinct ideas within it.
3. Separation of those ideas into standalone pieces.
4. Editing each piece so that it can be understood independently.

## How Someone Could Build It

A creator could:

1. Take a long-form source.
2. Identify every genuinely distinct idea.
3. Remove dependencies on surrounding text where necessary.
4. Turn each idea into a standalone short-form post.
5. Preserve the relationship between the extracted post and its original source where useful.

## Resources / Research

The method developed from the Wild Womaniya content-development process.

No external research is added here.

## Open Questions

- How should genuinely distinct ideas be identified consistently?
- How much editing is appropriate before an extracted idea becomes a new piece?
- Should the source of each micro-post always be preserved?
- Could the process be automated?

## Risks / Limitations

Over-extraction can turn one idea into multiple artificial fragments.

The distinction between a standalone idea and a supporting point requires judgment.

## Development History

The method developed through the process of extracting short-form content from longer conversations and written material.

It became a distinct content-production method rather than merely an editing technique.

ADOPTED.

The method has been adopted as a content-development approach.
