# [ADOPTED] IDEA 005 — Pick One. Do It. Stop.

## Origin

This idea developed during the exploration of body-based tools for moments when everything feels like too much.

## The Problem

When someone is overwhelmed, being given too many things to do can itself become overwhelming.

A system intended to help may therefore need to reduce the number of decisions required from the person.

## The Idea

Instead of requiring someone to complete an entire sequence of practices, present multiple possible tools and allow the person to:

**Pick One. Do It. Stop.**

The person does not need to complete every tool or continue beyond the selected practice.

## Why This Idea Exists

The principle was developed as part of the Body-First Calm approach and the 11-Tool Standalone Body Calm Toolkit.

It creates a low-demand way of interacting with a collection of tools.

## Who Is It For?

### Primary

People experiencing overwhelm who may benefit from having one simple action rather than a list of tasks.

### Secondary

Not yet defined.

## Context

The principle is connected to:

- Body-First Calm
- 11-Tool Standalone Body Calm Toolkit

## What Already Exists

The phrase and principle were established during development of the Body-First Calm toolkit.

## What Makes This Idea Distinct

The user is explicitly not required to work through the entire toolkit.

The system gives permission to select one option, complete it, and stop.

## What Has Already Been Done

- The principle was identified.
- It was incorporated into the conceptual structure of the Body-First Calm toolkit.
- It was connected to the broader approach of reducing overwhelm.

## What Has NOT Been Done

No separate standalone product implementing this principle has been documented.

## Minimum Viable Version

A minimum version would present several possible tools and clearly communicate:

**Pick one. Do it. Stop.**

## How Someone Could Build It

A person could apply the principle to a toolkit, printable, card deck, app, guide, or other collection of practices.

Each available option could be presented independently, without requiring completion of the remaining options.

## Resources / Research

The idea developed from the Body-First Calm toolkit conversations.

No external research is added here.

## Open Questions

- Should the principle appear as an instruction at the beginning of the toolkit?
- Should it also be repeated within individual tools?
- What other formats could use the principle?

## Risks / Limitations

The principle is a design approach rather than a claim that completing one activity will necessarily resolve overwhelm.

## Development History

The principle emerged while considering how an 11-tool toolkit could remain usable when the person using it was already overwhelmed.

It became a distinct principle: the user can select one tool rather than being asked to work through the entire collection.

## Current State

ADOPTED.

The principle has been adopted as part of the Body-First Calm toolkit direction.
