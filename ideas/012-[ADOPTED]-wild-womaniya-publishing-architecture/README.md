## Origin

This idea developed from the need to organize Wild Womaniya's written work and products into different levels of depth and format.

## The Problem

Different kinds of written material can serve different purposes.

A short practical resource does not need to be structured like a long handbook, while a substantial body of work may require a different format altogether.

## The Idea

Create a publishing architecture with distinct levels of written work:

### Free Field Guides

Short practical resources, approximately 3–10 pages.

### Paid Companions

More developed handbooks, approximately 15–40 pages.

### Premium Conversations

Long-form works of approximately 50–200+ pages.

The architecture allows different levels of depth to exist within the same publishing ecosystem.

## Why This Idea Exists

The structure provides a way to distinguish short, accessible resources from more substantial paid and long-form works.

## Who Is It For?

### Primary

Readers and customers of Wild Womaniya's written resources.

### Secondary

Not defined.

## Context

The publishing architecture was developed as part of Wild Womaniya Studio and its broader product and publishing ecosystem.

## What Already Exists

The three-level publishing structure was defined:

- Free Field Guides
- Paid Companions
- Premium Conversations

## What Makes This Idea Distinct

The distinction is based primarily on depth, length, and level of development rather than treating every written work as the same type of publication.

## What Has Already Been Done

- The three publishing levels were defined.
- Approximate page ranges were established.
- The architecture was incorporated into the Wild Womaniya publishing direction.

## What Has NOT Been Done

The complete catalog of publications and the final publishing workflow were not documented here.

## Minimum Viable Version

A minimum version consists of the three publishing categories and their intended ranges:

- Free Field Guides — 3–10 pages.
- Paid Companions — 15–40 pages.
- Premium Conversations — 50–200+ pages.

## How Someone Could Build It

A publisher could classify each new work according to its intended depth and format.

The work could then be created and distributed according to its category.

The specific publishing platform was not fixed.

## Resources / Research

The architecture was developed through the Wild Womaniya publishing discussions.

No external research is added here.

## Open Questions

- What qualifies a work to move from one level to another?
- Should page count remain the primary distinction?
- What pricing structure should accompany each level?
- Should all three levels use the same visual identity?
- How should publications be organized in the public catalog?

## Risks / Limitations

Page count alone does not necessarily determine the depth or usefulness of a publication.

The categories therefore represent an organizational structure rather than a quality ranking.

## Development History

The architecture emerged while developing Wild Womaniya's publishing direction.

The work was divided into short free resources, medium-length paid handbooks, and long-form premium works.

## Current State

ADOPTED.

The publishing architecture was adopted as a framework for Wild Womaniya's written work.
